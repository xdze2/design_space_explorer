**Why choose it.** A rigid rod links the two side belts of the Y axis so that one motor drives both sides in lockstep. It is the cheapest cure for racking: one motor, one driver, nothing to synchronize electronically.

**Tradeoffs.** The rod adds a little rotating mass and needs to be straight and well supported. It only helps if both belts are tensioned equally, and it forces both sides to move together, so any binding on one rail is felt on the other. The rotating mass of the rod slows acceleration slightly.

**Links.** It requires a belt drive (GT2 belt), since the rod works by tying the two Y belts together.
