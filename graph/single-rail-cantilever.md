**Why choose it.** One Y rail along one side carries a cantilevered X beam. There is no parallelism to get right, no racking, a single belt and motor, less extrusion and moving mass, and one side stays open for loading paper or odd-shaped media. Many commercial pen plotters work this way.

**Tradeoffs.** It brings two sub-functions, the way motion is transmitted and the way the axes are guided; the guide is critical here because the single Y guide takes the whole tilting moment. The load sits off the rail, so the carriage sees a tilting moment: beam sag and twist, higher wheel loads and a lower resonance, which limits acceleration. It needs a wide carriage (four or more wheels or two bearing blocks), a stiff beam (a deeper section rather than a thin one) and a short overhang. A long overhang of thin extrusion flexes noticeably on a large sheet.
