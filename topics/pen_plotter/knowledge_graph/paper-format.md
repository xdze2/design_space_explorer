**The limit.** The size of the sheet the machine has to cover sets the travel of both axes, and with it the length of every belt, rail and beam. Each extra bit of travel makes the beam longer and so heavier or less stiff, lowers the resonance and costs money in extrusion.

**How solutions fare.** Layouts that pile load on a long beam suffer first: a cantilever with a long overhang flexes, a single belt run stretches more. Two-sided and CoreXY layouts hold up better because both beam ends are supported. Leadscrews become slow and whippy as travel grows. A polargraph grows cheaply but loses accuracy at its edges, a grit wheel takes long rolls but is no wider than its carriage, and an arm stays small. The target sheet itself belongs to a design document, not to the graph.
