**Why choose it.** Two motors at the top corners of a wall board, with belts or cords down to a hanging gondola that holds the pen. The cheapest way to a large drawing area (move the motors apart and use longer belts), and it takes almost no floor space.

**Tradeoffs.** The gondola swings, so it must draw slowly. Cords stretch and gravity pulls, so accuracy is worst at the edges (one build reports 1-2 mm), and the top of the board is out of reach. Straight lines must be split into short segments or they bow. The board is part of the machine, so it brings no guides and no separate way to hold the surface, only the drive and the transmission. The pen lift usually tilts the whole gondola off the wall, which limits the choice of tools. Builders call it an art machine rather than a precision plotter.

**Links.** It excludes the pen weight on a free slide: on a vertical surface gravity no longer presses the pen. It excludes the leadscrew: the travel is made by belts or cords.
