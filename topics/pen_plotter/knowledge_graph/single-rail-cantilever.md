**Why choose it.** One Y rail along one side carries a cantilevered X beam. There is no parallelism to get right, no racking, a single belt and motor, less extrusion and moving mass, and one side stays open for loading paper or odd-shaped media. Many commercial pen plotters work this way.

**Tradeoffs.** The single Y guide takes the whole tilting moment: beam sag and twist, higher wheel loads and a lower resonance, which limits acceleration. It needs a wide carriage (four or more wheels or two bearing blocks), a stiff beam (a deeper section rather than a thin one) and a short overhang. A long overhang of thin extrusion flexes noticeably on a large sheet: the EasyDraw went from a cantilever (V3, A4 only) to a frame supported on all four sides (V4) to reach A3 and A2. It brings the drive, the transmission, the guides and a way to hold the surface. One rail, one belt and one motor also make it cheap.
